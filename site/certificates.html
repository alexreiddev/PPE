Reading the markings

What the codes on the strip mean.

A short guide for site teams checking kit against a specification. The manufacturer’s certificate is always the reference.

EN 388

Gloves — mechanical risks

Six characters, read left to right. On a glove marked 4243BP:

1st digit
Abrasion, level 1–4
2nd digit
Blade cut (coupe test), level 1–5
3rd digit
Tear, level 1–4
4th digit
Puncture, level 1–4
Letter
ISO cut, A (lowest) to F (highest)
P
Passed the impact test. No letter means not claimed
X
Not tested or not applicable
ANSI/ISEA 138

Gloves — back-of-hand impact

Tests the force that reaches the knuckles and fingers when a 5 J impact lands on the back of the hand. Level 1 is the lowest rating and level 3 the highest.

Level 1
Average transmitted force ≤ 9 kN
Level 2
Average transmitted force ≤ 6.5 kN
Level 3
Average transmitted force ≤ 4 kN
EN ISO 20345  /  ASTM F2413

Safety footwear

EN ISO 20345 is the European standard for safety footwear with a 200 J toe cap; ASTM F2413 is the US equivalent. The letters on the strip are the extra protections claimed.

EH
Electrical hazard protection
PR
Puncture-resistant sole
SR
Slip resistant
NT
Non-metallic toe cap
ATEX  /  IECEx  /  Zone 1/21

Hazardous-area devices

Equipment certified for use where an explosive atmosphere can occur. Check the zone on the device against the area classification on your permit.

Zone 1
Explosive gas atmosphere likely in normal operation
Zone 21
The same, for combustible dust
Div 1
The North American classification for the same conditions
